About me

I’m a PhD student in Data Science at the University of Rome Tor Vergata. I’m curious about what happens when AI agents tackle a problem together. Can one agent spot a gap in another’s reasoning? When they disagree, what evidence would help them settle it?

These questions shape my interests in generative AI, multi-agent systems, and scientific and logical reasoning. I’m also interested in how agents find the information they need through retrieval, and which reasoning skills a smaller model can learn from a larger one through knowledge distillation.

#

My background is in bioinformatics and clinical biostatistics. At Bambino Gesù Children’s Hospital, I worked with clinical researchers to analyse immune responses and protein profiles in paediatric cohorts.

What I’m working on

Reasoning about language. I use FrameNet to study how models interpret who did what to whom. I also investigate whether models can spot inconsistencies across contract clauses.

Knowledge distillation. Building on my work on Italian fact verification, I’d like to test whether a smaller model can learn one particularly useful judgment from a larger one: when there is enough evidence to draw a conclusion and when to leave the question open.

Agents for scientific reasoning. One direction I’d like to explore is a team of agents that propose competing hypotheses, look for relevant findings, and identify an observation that would distinguish one explanation from another.

Retrieval across text and protein sequences. I’m exploring two ways to search Swiss-Prot for related proteins: by their amino acid sequences and by the descriptions of their biological functions. I’m interested in where the two searches agree and what each one finds that the other misses.
