About me

I’m a PhD student in Data Science at the University of Rome Tor Vergata. I’m curious about what happens when AI agents tackle a problem together. Can one agent spot a gap in another’s reasoning? When they disagree, what evidence would help them settle it?

These questions shape my interests in generative AI, multi-agent systems, and scientific and logical reasoning. I’m also interested in how agents find the information they need through retrieval, and which reasoning skills a smaller model can learn from a larger one through knowledge distillation.

Featured project

NL4AI 2026 · Perugia, Italy

Scale or Specialization?

We tested how well language models verify claims in Italian. On FEVER-IT, adapting Llama 3.1 8B raised accuracy from 70.67% to 88.85%. The largest gain came on claims where the evidence was too thin to reach a verdict.

What I’m working on

Knowledge distillation

My work on Italian fact verification sparked a broader question: which decisions can a small model learn from a larger teacher? I’d like to apply distillation to ranking passages for retrieval, spotting conflicts between contract clauses, and choosing when an agent should ask for more evidence. I want to measure how well those skills transfer to unfamiliar examples and how much they cost to run.

Multi-agent reasoning

I’d like to explore how agents could test competing scientific hypotheses. One could propose an explanation, another look for a counterexample, and a third identify the observation that would distinguish between them.

Reasoning about language

I use FrameNet to study how models interpret who did what to whom. I also investigate whether models can spot inconsistencies across contract clauses.

Retrieval across text and protein sequences

I’m exploring two ways to search Swiss-Prot for related proteins: by amino acid sequence and by the descriptions of their biological functions. Where do the two searches agree, and what does each one find that the other misses?
